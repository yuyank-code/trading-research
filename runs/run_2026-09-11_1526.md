# Research Run — 2026-09-11 15:26

## Meaningful change
Audited the authoritative `bitcoin-ml-trading/production_research.py`. The execution engine still uses next-bar OHLC and stop-first resolution when both stop and target are touched. That ordering is not observable from OHLC alone.

Implemented commit `a325528d7d7eef1a893b0bc127f0be352f39ae1d` to expose this uncertainty in the trade artifact:
- each trade now records `ambiguous_intrabar` when both stop and target are inside the same bar;
- aggregate metrics now report `ambiguous_intrabar_trades` and `ambiguous_intrabar_fraction`;
- existing stop-first execution is preserved as the conservative baseline, but the ambiguity is no longer invisible.

This is instrumentation, not a profitability claim. The next experiment must compare stop-first, target-first, exclusion, and lower-timeframe replay where data permit.

## New literature
1. Huang, Wang & Jiang (2026), *Research on Machine Learning High-Frequency Trading Strategies Under Transaction Cost*. The study explicitly models fees, bid-ask spreads and slippage and reports a prediction-to-net-return disconnect; it argues that cost-aware filtering can matter more than deeper architectures and warns that single-path returns can be unstable. DOI: 10.71411/eaou.2026.v2i4.1672.
2. Huang, Wang & Jiang (2026), *Research on Machine Learning High-Frequency Trading Strategy of Cryptocurrency Based on Transaction Cost-Aware Filtering*. BTC/USDT hourly study using 10 bps full-dimensional costs and 27-fold non-anchored WFO; naive strategies deteriorate sharply after costs and the cost-aware filter reduces turnover. Reported returns are descriptive and should not be treated as evidence of dominance without independent significance tests.
3. Santoni, Jouanne & Scullin (2026), *Equity Strategy Backtesting: Luck or Edge? The MinervaScore as a Statistical Robustness Grade*. Combines DSR, PBO, SPA, minimum track record length and regime stability as an auditable validation layer. Importantly, the paper explicitly says the score is not a probability of future profitability.
4. Bailey & López de Prado (2014), *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*. Multiple-testing correction remains mandatory when selecting among many variants.
