## Pipeline change
Commit `bb58ffeabd3693e5940072b848f6907028df6446` in `yuyank-code/bitcoin-ml-trading` now decomposes execution costs in the production backtest into:
- commission fees
- entry slippage
- exit slippage
- total slippage cost

Trade records now also retain mid/reference entry and exit prices and gross PnL before slippage. This makes cost attribution auditable rather than treating slippage only as an implicit change to execution prices.

## Why this matters
Recent 2026 research continues to show a prediction-to-trading gap. Kim's VALID study reports that transaction costs consumed 55–91% of gross alpha across its crypto ML corpus and that AUC-only validation produced substantial false positives; CPCV+PBO reduced false positives in its controlled null experiments. Bysik & Ślepaczuk report that naive hourly BTC ML strategies failed at 10 bps while cost-aware filtering recovered selected configurations, without establishing formal model dominance. Huang et al. likewise report a disconnect between prediction accuracy and net returns and emphasize explicit fees, spreads and slippage.

## New hypothesis — H42: cost attribution integrity
For every promoted OOS trade, the accounting identity must hold:
`net_pnl = gross_pnl_no_slippage - fees - slippage_cost`.
Aggregate fee/slippage totals must equal the sum of trade-level components. Any mismatch invalidates the backtest artifact.

## Next decisive experiment
Run the existing frozen OOS predictions through an explicit cost grid and report both total net performance and the fee/slippage decomposition. Then align the executable holding/event horizon with the prediction label before any model promotion. Only after those gates should CPCV/PBO/DSR/SPA and an untouched holdout determine whether a candidate is credible.
