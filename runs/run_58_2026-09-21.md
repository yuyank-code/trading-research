## Meaningful progress

Added H123 and Research 72 to make **incremental net OOS value over strong frozen baselines** an explicit promotion gate. This is a material shift from comparing ML models against each other toward attributing economic value to the predictive component itself.

## Literature

- Bengoechea Pardo (2026), SSRN 6952859: a leak-free, cost-aware large-cap US equity benchmark reports that the best promoted ML model only marginally exceeded a no-ML Black-Litterman baseline while incurring more than three times cumulative transaction costs; an equal-weight baseline was also close. The reported attribution is that covariance regularization and Bayesian shrinkage, rather than predictive content, drove much of the risk-adjusted result.
- Saly-Kaufmann, Wood, Calliess & Zohren (2026): a large futures benchmark evaluates models using OOS significance, tail risk, break-even transaction costs, seed robustness and computational efficiency, and reports that some richer temporal models do beat linear baselines. This is useful counter-evidence: baseline dominance must be measured, not assumed.
- Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026): hourly BTC ML strategies can lose their economics under 10-bps costs; cost-aware filtering helps selected configurations, while bootstrap evidence does not establish formal XGBoost dominance.
- Belyakov (2026): market-neutral RL incorporates transaction costs directly into its reward and uses rolling walk-forward evaluation, supporting cost-aware objective design while remaining external evidence rather than proof of transferable alpha.
