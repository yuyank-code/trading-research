# Research Run — 2026-09-11 11:21 IST

## Meaningful finding

The main validation harness contained a misleading integrity diagnostic: `time_sorted_before_dedup` was calculated only after sorting the prediction frame, so it could never report that the input arrived out of chronological order. This was fixed in `yuyank-code/bitcoin-ml-trading` commit `3c961b0a18230b96cebb16ce6c504b5a51163ce3`.

The validator now also fails closed on missing labels/probabilities and non-finite probabilities, rather than allowing malformed prediction artifacts into classification metrics.

## Why this matters

A validation artifact must distinguish an ordering defect from a clean chronological prediction stream. Otherwise a generator that emits rows out of order can look valid after normalization. This is especially important for event-time purging, embargo, and trade alignment because chronological ordering is part of the validation contract.

## Literature update

Recent work reinforces the existing validation direction:

- Santoni, Jouanne & Scullin (2026), *Equity Strategy Backtesting: Luck or Edge?* proposes combining DSR, PBO, SPA and minimum track-record length with regime stability as a robustness reporting layer. The authors explicitly frame it as an auditable validation/reporting layer rather than proof of predictability.
- Kim (2026), *Beyond Accuracy: A Validation Framework for Machine Learning in Cryptocurrency Trading*, reports large gaps between predictive metrics and economic performance and emphasizes CPCV/PBO plus transaction-cost-aware validation.
- Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026), *Machine Learning-Based Bitcoin Trading Under Transaction Costs*, reports that naive hourly BTC ML trading fails at 10 bps in tested configurations while cost-aware forecast filtering can restore profitability in selected cases; it does not establish formal model dominance.
- Jacquier, Muhle-Karbe & Mulligan (2025), *In-Sample and Out-of-Sample Sharpe Ratios for Linear Predictive Models*, provides quantitative support for the idea that complexity and many weak signals can reduce OOS replication.
