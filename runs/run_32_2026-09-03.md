## Meaningful findings

1. The dedicated `runs/` directory exists, but only Run 22 and Run 31 are currently visible there. Earlier research commits used numbered files under `research/`, so the run history is incomplete in the dedicated run index. This is a provenance/documentation gap, not trading evidence.

2. White's Reality Check and Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability (SPA) are appropriate additions to the final model-selection gate. They evaluate a family of candidate strategies rather than treating the selected winner as if it had been chosen a priori. Hsu & Kuan (2005) demonstrate the practical use of both tests for technical-trading rule universes; Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1998) establish the importance of correcting for data snooping across a rule universe.

3. DSR remains necessary but is not sufficient. Bailey & Lopez de Prado show that the selected Sharpe is inflated by multiple testing and non-normality. SPA/Reality Check adds a complementary family-level test of superior predictive ability. PBO/CSCV addresses the probability that selection itself is overfit.

4. Recent 2026 crypto-ML evidence continues to show a prediction-to-trading disconnect: predictive metrics can look strong while transaction costs, spread and slippage erase economic value. Therefore the primary economic target should be net executable return and turnover, with accuracy/AUC treated only as diagnostics.

5. The current research architecture should not use a composite robustness score as a substitute for individual statistical gates. A recent 2026 MinervaScore paper explicitly positions its score as a reporting/validation layer and reports no significant forward relationship in its preregistered unseen-market test.

## New hypotheses

### H23 — Family-level superior predictive ability
After the candidate family and trial registry are frozen, the selected strategy must beat the relevant benchmark under a cost-aware SPA/Reality Check. A positive individual Sharpe without family-level significance is insufficient.

### H24 — Economic ranking survives correction
The ranking of candidate models by net OOS performance should remain reasonably stable after applying realistic costs and search-adjusted inference. If ranking reverses completely, the apparent model advantage is likely execution-friction sensitive.

### H25 — Turnover-cost elasticity
The marginal deterioration in net performance as execution costs increase should be positively related to turnover. A strategy with unusually low sensitivity despite high turnover requires an explicit execution explanation and audit.

### H26 — Frozen-threshold integrity
Signal thresholds must be selected only from development/CV data. Re-optimizing thresholds on OOS returns converts the OOS period into another training/selection sample and invalidates its interpretation.
