# Research Run — 2026-09-11 09:22

## Meaningful literature update
Fresh 2026 evidence continues to support a strict economic-validation gate rather than model-complexity escalation:

- Kim, *Beyond Accuracy: A Validation Framework for Machine Learning in Cryptocurrency Trading* (SSRN, revised Aug. 2026): 340 strategy variants; reports 27% false-positive rate for AUC-only validation in its Monte Carlo null experiments, reduced to 0% with CPCV + PBO, and reports transaction costs consuming 55–91% of gross alpha. Treat as a preprint, not definitive ground truth, but highly relevant to the project's validation design.
- Bysik & Ślepaczuk, *Machine Learning-Based Bitcoin Trading Under Transaction Costs* (arXiv, May 2026): ~70k hourly BTC-USDT observations, 27 WFO folds; naive sign strategies fail at 10 bps while cost-aware forecast-magnitude filtering helps selected configurations; bootstrap evidence does not establish formal model dominance.
- Pindza, *Microstructure alpha: hierarchical learning and cross-asset transfer in cryptocurrency markets* (Frontiers in Blockchain, June 2026): >3m minute observations; purged walk-forward evaluation finds microstructure information but reports gradient-boosting overfit under leakage controls and no strategy surviving realistic exchange fees. This is particularly relevant because it argues that information can be real yet economically untradeable.
- Jacquier, Muhle-Karbe & Mulligan, *In-Sample and Out-of-Sample Sharpe Ratios for Linear Predictive Models* (2025): analytically shows OOS replication deteriorates with strategy complexity and many weak signals, while more training data improves replication. This supports keeping the current simple-model ensemble as the baseline and requiring complexity to demonstrate incremental OOS value.
- Bailey & López de Prado, *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio* (2014): DSR explicitly adjusts for multiple testing and non-normal returns; it should be applied to any selected strategy only after the complete trial count is frozen.

## New testable hypothesis
**H41 — Breakeven-cost stability:** If the candidate has genuine economic edge, its OOS performance should degrade monotonically as execution costs increase and retain positive expectancy up to a measurable breakeven cost. A strategy that only wins at one narrowly chosen cost assumption is not robust.

Predeclared test:
1. Freeze the candidate/model/policy using development data only.
2. Evaluate the untouched OOS stream at a cost grid of 0.5x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x and 3x the baseline explicit fee+slippage assumption.
3. Record net CAGR, Sharpe, max drawdown, expectancy, turnover and breakeven cost.
4. Require monotonic degradation and report the entire cost-response curve; do not select the best cost point.
5. Apply DSR/PBO/SPA only after the candidate is frozen and the complete trial registry is available.
