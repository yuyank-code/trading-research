3. New literature strengthens the cost/execution gate. Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026) evaluate ~70,000 hourly BTC-USDT observations with walk-forward ML and find naive sign strategies fail at 10 bps; cost-aware forecast filtering rescues selected configurations, while formal model dominance is not established. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060

4. Dashyan (2026) documents a crypto perpetual-futures backtest-to-live gap where a selected high-Sharpe configuration was not representative of robust performance; the paper emphasizes selection bias, execution adverse selection, and search-adjusted inference. https://ssrn.com/abstract=7353678

5. Bailey et al. establish that increasing the number of tested configurations increases backtest-overfitting risk; PBO/CSCV was designed to quantify that risk. https://ssrn.com/abstract=2326253

6. Mulligan, Jacquier & Muhle-Karbe (2025, revised 2025) show that OOS Sharpe replication deteriorates with strategy complexity and many weak signals, and improves with more training data. https://ssrn.com/abstract=5086171

7. López de Prado & Porcu (2025) provide a recent search-adjusted DSR framework addressing selection bias, backtest overfitting and non-normality. https://ssrn.com/abstract=7198158

## New hypothesis

### H49 — Search-adjusted incremental edge

A candidate must demonstrate incremental predictive/trading value against the naive benchmark after accounting for the full number of materially tried configurations. The final decision must report raw OOS metrics, benchmark-relative tests, PBO/DSR/SPA-style search adjustment, and cost-stress results. A single best Sharpe cell is not sufficient evidence.
