# H58 — Family-level Reality Check and Superior Predictive Ability gate

## Status
Proposed / not yet empirically evaluated.

## Question
Does the candidate strategy family contain evidence of economic value after accounting for the fact that the research process searched across many correlated models, features, parameters, horizons, thresholds, and execution rules?

## Motivation
A strong individual backtest is not sufficient when many related alternatives were searched. White's Reality Check and Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability (SPA) test provide family-level corrections for data snooping. Recent financial-ML work also shows that standard validation can produce false positives when the workflow is adaptive, strengthening the case for testing the selected family rather than only its winner.

## Falsifiable hypothesis
After preserving the complete candidate/trial universe and freezing the evaluation protocol, the best candidate's net OOS performance will remain distinguishable from the benchmark under a family-level Reality Check / SPA procedure and will not be explained by the same search process applied to zero-alpha null data.

## Experimental protocol
1. Define the benchmark before inspecting the final confirmation period.
2. Preserve every candidate trial, including failed models, parameter variants, thresholds, horizons, and execution policies.
3. Use purged/embargoed walk-forward OOS predictions generated without future information.
4. Evaluate net returns after explicit spread, commission, slippage, and market-impact assumptions where applicable.
5. Compute the performance differential of every candidate against the benchmark on the same OOS observations.
6. Apply a dependence-preserving bootstrap for White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA; do not treat independent observations as an assumption for serially dependent returns.
7. Report the raw best-candidate statistic, family-adjusted p-values, bootstrap confidence intervals, effective number/correlation structure of trials, and benchmark-relative economic metrics.
8. Repeat the complete candidate-selection procedure on zero-alpha synthetic/null data using the same search budget and selection rules.
9. Stress costs and slippage by +25%, +50%, and +100% and report whether family-level evidence survives.
10. Keep a final untouched confirmation period outside all model, threshold, execution, and statistical-test calibration.

## Failure conditions
- Family-adjusted evidence does not reject the benchmark/null at the pre-registered level.
- Apparent significance disappears after dependence-aware bootstrap correction.
- Results rely on a single narrow candidate while nearby candidates fail.
- Performance collapses under modest cost/slippage stress.
- Null-data searches repeatedly produce similarly strong winners.
- Any leakage, label overlap, benchmark contamination, or post-hoc candidate exclusion is detected.

## Promotion standard
H58 is a family-level statistical gate, not standalone evidence of profitability. A candidate must also pass the project's leakage, workflow-null, selection-stability, parameter-robustness, minimum-track-record, and final-confirmation requirements.

## Evidence standard
This hypothesis is a test design. Numerical claims require reproducible OOS return vectors, benchmark vectors, trial registry, bootstrap configuration, data/version hashes, and committed output artifacts.
