# H57 — Pre-registered cost-survival and parameter-plateau test

## Status
Proposed / not yet empirically evaluated.

## Question
Does a candidate trading rule retain positive, risk-adjusted out-of-sample performance across a pre-registered transaction-cost/slippage surface and a neighborhood of nearby parameter values, without materially increasing turnover?

## Motivation
Recent controlled research continues to show that transaction costs can reverse the ranking and profitability of trading models, while backtest selection among many variants creates a multiple-testing problem. A parameter plateau is useful only if it is evaluated out of sample; a visually smooth in-sample optimum is not evidence of robustness by itself.

## Falsifiable hypothesis
After the model, feature set, execution rule, and cost model are frozen, candidates whose performance survives progressively harsher friction assumptions and nearby parameter perturbations will have better OOS stability than candidates selected solely by peak in-sample Sharpe.

## Experimental protocol
1. Freeze the feature and model family before inspecting the final confirmation period.
2. Define the parameter neighborhood and friction grid before evaluation.
3. Fit all stateful preprocessing only within each training fold.
4. Use purged/embargoed OOS splits with purge at least as large as the maximum label horizon.
5. For each OOS path, evaluate baseline friction plus +25%, +50%, and +100% adverse cost/slippage stress.
6. Evaluate the selected configuration and its pre-defined local parameter neighborhood; do not expand the neighborhood after seeing OOS results.
7. Record net return, Sharpe, Sortino, maximum drawdown, turnover, trade count, abstention rate, and break-even cost.
8. Preserve every trial in the registry and apply selection-aware statistics (DSR/PBO where applicable).
9. Repeat the entire selection procedure on zero-alpha synthetic/null data with the same search budget.
10. Keep a final untouched confirmation period that is never used for model, parameter, threshold, or execution-policy selection.

## Failure conditions
- Net OOS Sharpe becomes non-positive at the reference friction.
- Performance depends on a narrow parameter peak rather than a pre-registered neighborhood.
- A small increase in costs causes a qualitative collapse in performance.
- The same search procedure frequently selects apparently profitable null strategies.
- Leakage, label overlap, or preprocessing contamination is detected.
- Results cannot be reproduced from the recorded data/version/configuration hashes.

## Promotion standard
No promotion from H57 alone. A candidate must also pass leakage audit, workflow-level null falsification, selection-stability analysis, and the project's minimum track-record / multiple-testing requirements.

## Evidence standard
This hypothesis is a test design, not a claim of profitability. Any numerical result must be generated from the frozen experiment and committed with inputs, configuration, trial registry, and reproducible output artifacts.
