# H130 — Cost-Aware Decision Objective vs Point-Prediction Optimization

## Status
PROPOSED — not promoted.

## Hypothesis
Optimizing a forecast model for downstream net portfolio utility can outperform optimizing pointwise prediction loss, but only if the improvement survives strict point-in-time validation, realistic execution costs, and a frozen final holdout.

## Motivation
Recent 2026 work emphasizes a recurring prediction-to-trading gap. A cost-aware BTC walk-forward study reports that naive sign trading can fail after 10 bps while a cost-aware execution filter can restore economics in selected configurations. A 2026 Smart Predict-then-Optimize paper argues that forecast accuracy need not map monotonically to portfolio decision quality when trading frictions and constraints matter. A separate large-cap US equity benchmark reports a negative result in which an ML model only marginally beat a no-ML portfolio while incurring substantially higher transaction costs.

## Controlled comparison
A. Pointwise objective: minimize predictive loss / maximize forecast fit.
B. Decision-aware objective: optimize a pre-declared downstream utility surrogate including turnover/cost penalties.
C. Strong non-ML baseline with identical portfolio and execution machinery.

No candidate may change universe, rebalance schedule, risk limits, or execution assumptions between A/B/C.

## Leakage controls
- Point-in-time feature availability.
- Purge and embargo according to label horizon.
- All normalization, feature selection, and cost-threshold calibration fit inside training folds.
- No final-OOS feedback.
- Preserve model/version/fold identifiers.

## Economic tests
Evaluate gross and net results with commission, spread, slippage, nonlinear impact where supported, borrow/funding where relevant, turnover and capacity constraints. Stress costs at 1x, 1.5x and 2x and perturb execution lag.

## Statistical tests
Compare paired OOS net returns against the frozen baseline. Report confidence intervals, deflated/selection-aware evidence where applicable, subperiod/regime stability, and placebo controls. A point estimate alone is insufficient.

## Promotion gate
Promote only if B provides incremental net OOS utility over A and C, survives cost stress and execution perturbation, does not rely on a single regime, and passes the repository's immutable OOS accounting contract.

## Current blocker
The repository README still identifies forward-label overlap at walk-forward boundaries as unresolved, and no immutable prediction-to-net-return OOS artifact is currently available. Therefore H130 has no numerical result yet.
